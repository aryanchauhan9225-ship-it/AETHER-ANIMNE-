# THE STARS ARE ALREADY DEAD? — क्या आसमान के तारे अब भी ज़िंदा हैं?

60 s cosmic science documentary, Hindi narration. 1920×1080, **24 fps** (1,440 frames).

**Reference video:** it did not arrive in this session; no video file was uploaded. The visual language was built from the written analysis in the brief: deep black, dense fine stars, antique-gold and warm-white line illustrations, a minimal silhouette, thin diagrams, elegant serif type and small subtitles.

## Scenes

1. **0–9.9 · The mystery.**
   - Black, then one golden star at 0.4 s; thousands follow.
   - A thin gold figure is drawn under the sky on a faint horizon, and the camera creeps forward.
   - Title **THE STARS ARE ALREADY DEAD?** / क्या आसमान के तारे अब भी ज़िंदा हैं?, then **ARE WE SEEING THE PAST?**
2. **9.9–24.4 · Light begins its journey.**
   - A distant star with fine spikes; a line-art Earth draws itself.
   - A light pulse leaves at 10.6 s, crosses on a ticked scale and reaches Earth at 17.2 s (end of v04). An arrival ring forms and the day side lights.
   - **LIGHT TRAVELS THROUGH SPACE.**
   - Readings: *LIGHT LEAVES THE STAR — emitted long ago* and *LIGHT ARRIVES — what we see, now*; then **WHAT WE SEE IS OLD LIGHT.**
3. **24.4–30.7 · The Sun.**
   - A stippled Sun (limb darkening, granulation shimmer, fine corona rays), Earth on its orbit arc and the line between them.
   - A photon crosses while a clock counts 0:00 → 8:20 on a timeline.
   - **SUNLIGHT / 8 MINUTES 20 SECONDS** (lands on "आठ मिनट बीस सेकंड").
   - Readings: *the Sun, our nearest star*; *Earth ≈ 150 million km away*; *light ≈ 300,000 km every second*.
4. **30.7–37.3 · Light-years.**
   - A dive deeper through the field (shutter streaks).
   - A logarithmic distance ruler marked in light-years. Stars at real distances: Proxima Centauri 4.2 ly, Sirius 8.6 ly, plus 100 and 1,000 ly.
   - Light trails flow into the observer's eye.
   - **LIGHT-YEARS** — *a light-year is a distance: how far light travels in one year ≈ 9.46 trillion km*; *light from 1,000 light-years away set out about 1,000 years ago*.
5. **37.3–43.0 · The cosmic past.**
   - A stippled star; a timeline from year 0 to year 1,000 (*example: a star 1,000 light-years away*).
   - A packet carries a small image of the star as it was.
   - Meanwhile the source swells and cools to bronze, labelled *THE STAR NOW — it may have changed, or may not*. The narration says not every star is dead.
   - **WE SEE THE LIGHT FROM ITS PAST.**
6. **43.0–48.5 · The reveal.**
   - The camera pulls back as thousands of stars brighten; a tiny figure.
   - Shells of equal light-travel time draw around it: 4 years ago, 100, 1,000, 10,000.
   - **LOOKING FARTHER INTO SPACE / is looking further back in time** on the line "अंतरिक्ष में देखना, दरअसल समय में पीछे देखना भी है।"
7. **48.5–60 · Final.**
   - Back to the figure, which lifts its gaze as stars glow up around it.
   - **WE ARE LOOKING AT THE PAST.** / हम ब्रह्मांड का अतीत देख रहे हैं। (with v13)
   - Black, and the last gold particles drift up and go out.

## Sound (`mix.py`)

**Score:** original, built in code.
- A D-Aeolian pad that evolves and opens at the reveal.
- Sampled contrabass and cello pedals (VSCO 2 CE), faint high violin glints and a violin swell at the reveal.
- A sparse Salamander piano with long reverb that grows through the film.
- A controlled D1 sub-bass.
- A D-major chord on the final words.

**Effects:**
- shimmers for the first star, the titles, the star marks and the shells
- a rising tone along the light beam with a chime on arrival
- swishes for the photons
- low booms for the transitions, the dive and the reveal
- a descending sparkle for the final particle fade
- very low space "air"

**Mix:** music ducked about 4 dB under the voice; −14 LUFS master.
